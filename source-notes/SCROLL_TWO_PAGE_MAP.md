## Editorially difficult or sensitive passages

| PDF page | Source wording or issue | Editorial handling |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | “when the two practices are perfectly illuminated” | The pair is not named. The companion does not guess a technical list. |
| 12 | “contemplation of of impurities” | The repeated “of” is marked as an apparent typo. The contemplation is explained without treating bodies or people as morally dirty. |
| 18 | A harmful person appears comfortable while a virtuous person suffers | Explained as a warning that immediate appearances are not a complete moral scoreboard, not a licence to diagnose anyone's past karma. |
| 21 | “unrestrained in sexual desires after puberty” | Read as a general confession of conduct driven by compulsive desire, not a claim that puberty itself is an offence. |
| 23 to 31 | Killing linked with illness and shortened life | Retained as traditional consequence language with an explicit warning against medical or moral diagnosis of a suffering person. |
| 39 | A man's wife being unfaithful as a result; “five kinds of people” | The historical gender frame is named. The unidentified classification is not mapped onto current sexual orientations, gender identities or bodies. |
| 39 | Forced acts, voyeurism, humiliation and violation of celibacy | The companion centres coercion, safety, agency, trust and power. It does not require a victim to reconcile or make contact. |
| 40 | Birth “through transformation instead of from a womb” | Explained as sacred rebirth imagery and freedom from compulsive desire, not condemnation of pregnancy or bodies. |
| 44 | “idle chatter” alongside embellished writing and sexual themes | Explained through intention, impact and heedlessness, not as a ban on all humour, art or ordinary conversation. |
| 50 | Seven Buddhas mantra transliteration | Individual syllables are not forced into a speculative complete translation; the title and ritual function remain controlling. |
