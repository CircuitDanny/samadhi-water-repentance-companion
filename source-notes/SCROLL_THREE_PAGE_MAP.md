## Editorially difficult or sensitive passages

| PDF page | Source wording or issue | Editorial handling |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | “our third practice, our third cleansing” | The Chinese 三熏三沐 is an idiom of repeated perfuming and bathing as reverent preparation, not a technical third practice. The third scroll is named separately. Checked against the Taiwan Ministry of Education dictionary. |
| 14 | A person without remorse and shame is “no different from birds and beasts” | Identified as forceful premodern rhetoric about heedlessness. It is not repeated as permission to dehumanise people or demean animals. |
| 15 | Five pungent vegetables create a stench around scriptures, images and the assembly | Kept in its immediate ritual and East Asian dietary context, without turning it into a universal medical or lay rule. |
| 23 | Udrakarāmaputra's advanced concentration and later animal birth | Presented as a traditional example reported by the liturgy, not as a historically verified biography. |
| 24 to 29 | Vast hell dimensions, durations and graphic punishments | Measurements are treated as scale imagery rather than converted into false modern precision. The transformation vow controls the section's practical direction. |
| 34 to 36 | Ghosts, spirits and asuras associated with “hideousness” | Marked as inherited karmic and aesthetic language, not evidence that appearance reveals character or past conduct. |
| 37 to 40 | Disability, illness, poverty, separation and disaster listed as residual results | The traditional cosmology is explained with an explicit prohibition on victim-blaming or individual diagnosis and a direction towards practical care. |
| 43 | Seven Buddhas mantra transliteration | Explained by title and ritual function; uncertain transmitted syllables are not given an invented complete translation. |
| 47 to 51 | Memorial dhāraṇīs and Pure Land aspiration | Secure words and ritual functions are explained while uncertain grammar remains labelled. The guide does not claim certainty about a particular deceased person's state. |
