## Editorially difficult phrases

These source phrases are explained rather than silently normalised:

| PDF page | Source wording | Editorial handling |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | “proper sensation” as a definition of samādhi | Explained as collected awareness that does not chase sensory experience. The wording is not treated as a standard dictionary definition. |
| 3 | “no part of it remains cleansed” | Marked as an apparent typo for “uncleansed”; the next two washing comparisons confirm the direction of meaning. |
| 3 | “square inch of space” | Treated as compressed wording that may point to the mind or heart as the locus of reform. No precise technical claim is supplied. |
| 3 | “specs of dust” | Marked as an apparent typo for “specks of dust”. |
| 8 | “The Honored One practiced kindness and reverently record this” | Identified as grammatically incomplete. The surrounding ritual movement is explained without inventing a missing subject or verb. |
| 15 | “nine obscurities covering the land” | Kept as a broad image of darkness dispelled by teaching because the source does not enumerate nine items. |
| 19 | “we gave rise to [self-]nature without knowing” | Treated cautiously as deluded self-making or ignorance of true nature. No single technical interpretation is asserted. |
| 28 | “permanence, bliss, self-nature, and purity” | Explained as Mahāyāna nirvāṇa language, not an ordinary eternal ego. |
| 37 | “Eight Defilements” | Explained as a broad set of mental stains; no disputed eightfold list is presented as certain. |
| 38 | “Nine Conditions” | Given a practical meaning about the conditions supporting confused consciousness; the possible technical list is marked uncertain. |
| 39 | “Three Vows” | Left unenumerated because the source does not name the three. |
| 40 | “Dhama Body” | Marked as an apparent typo for “Dharma Body”. |
| 50 | “six senses as a formation of emptiness” | Explained as a warning against clinging to conditioned sense processes, not rejection of sensory life. |
| 51, 57 to 58, 61 to 62 | Sanskrit-derived mantras and dhāraṇīs | Their ritual purpose is explained. No complete word-for-word translation is invented where the transmitted grammar is uncertain. |
| 8, 59 to 64 | Precious Uḍumbara Blossom and Pure Cool Ground Bodhisattva-Mahāsattvas | Treated as ritual or collective invocations because no secure individual biography is supplied. |
