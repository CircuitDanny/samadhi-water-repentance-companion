## Editorially difficult phrases

These source phrases are explained rather than silently normalised:

| Source wording | Editorial handling |
|---|---|
| “proper sensation” as a definition of samādhi | Explained as collected awareness that does not chase sensory experience. The wording is not treated as a standard dictionary definition. |
| “no part of it remains cleansed” | Likely requires “uncleansed” from context. The guide uses the intended washing image without reproducing the error. |
| “we gave rise to [self-]nature without knowing” | Treated cautiously as deluded self-making or ignorance of true nature. No single technical interpretation is asserted. |
| “permanence, bliss, self-nature, and purity” | Explained as Mahāyāna nirvāṇa language, not an ordinary eternal ego. |
| “six senses as a formation of emptiness” | Explained as a warning against clinging to conditioned sense processes, not rejection of sensory life. |
| long Sanskrit-derived dhāraṇīs | Their ritual purpose is explained. No new word-for-word translation is invented. |
| Precious Uḍumbara Blossom and Pure Cool Ground Bodhisattva-Mahāsattvas | Treated as ritual or collective invocations because no secure individual biography is supplied. |
