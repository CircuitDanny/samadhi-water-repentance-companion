# Scroll companion revision evidence

## Scope and method

This note records the evidence used for the September 2026 expansion of the three standalone scroll companions. The controlling source is the English translation in Fo Guang Shan Hsi Lai Temple's 2022 electronic edition. Full page-delimited extracts were checked at:

- `reference/extracted/official-source__FGSHsiLaiTemple__WaterRepentance_SCROLL_01.txt`
- `reference/extracted/official-source__FGSHsiLaiTemple__WaterRepentance_SCROLL_02.txt`
- `reference/extracted/official-source__FGSHsiLaiTemple__WaterRepentance_SCROLL_03.txt`

The extracts sit outside this repository in the intake workspace. Page markers in those files are PDF page numbers. Scroll One's internal printed page 1 is PDF page 7. Scrolls Two and Three's internal printed page 1 is PDF page 3.

The complete source-liturgies and Samādhi analysis notes in `reference/reviews/` were read for structure and editorial risks. Claims were then checked against the page-delimited extracts. The stale combined Markdown was not used as an input to these revisions.

## External cross-checks

The following sources were opened or checked on 27 September 2026:

- Fo Guang Shan International Translation Center, [*The Buddhist Perspective on Life and Destiny*](https://www.fgsitc.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/A5-The-Buddhist-Perspective-on-Life-and-Destiny-2019.pdf). Used for the bounded point that habits, actions and present choices condition a life and can be changed. Cited in the Scroll Two companion.
- SuttaCentral, [Aṅguttara Nikāya 6.63, *A Penetrative Discourse*](https://suttacentral.net/an6.63/en/thanissaro). Used for the early Buddhist definition of karma through intention and its expression through body, speech and mind. Cited in the Scroll Two companion.
- BDK America, [*The Mahayana Sutra of Previous Lives and Contemplation of the Mind-ground*](https://bdkamerica.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/dBET_T0159_Previous-Lives_2021.pdf), translated text pp. 72–74. Checked for confession before the Three Jewels, resolve not to repeat an offence, shame and cleansing-water imagery. Cited in the Scroll Three companion.

These sources provide context, not a replacement translation of the Water Repentance.

The final editorial review also checked the [Taiwan Ministry of Education dictionary entry for 三熏三沐](https://dict.revised.moe.edu.tw/dictView.jsp?ID=144708&la=0&powerMode=0) against Scroll Three, PDF page 4. The idiom refers to repeated perfuming and bathing to show reverence. The companion and glossary now distinguish this from the separate mention of the third scroll. Suggested social applications after Hsing Yun's prayer are explicitly labelled as the companion's examples, rather than attributed to the prayer's exact words.

## Editorial decisions and remaining uncertainties

- Apparent English errors are named as apparent typos only where context is decisive: “cleansed” for “uncleansed”, “specs” for “specks”, repeated “of”, and “Dhama” for “Dharma”.
- Grammatically incomplete wording and unnamed technical sets remain uncertain. No correction is invented for “The Honored One practiced kindness and reverently record this”, “square inch of space”, “two practices”, “Three Vows”, “Nine Conditions” or “nine obscurities”.
- “Eight Defilements” has possible conventional explanations, but the source does not enumerate the intended eight. The companion uses the safe general sense of mental stains.
- The source's premodern language about sex, gender, bodies, disability, appearance and family relations is explained in context. It is not converted into new canonical claims or used to identify a person's past karma.
- Mantra and dhāraṇī material retains the existing cautious method: explain secure words, the title and ritual function, but do not manufacture a complete Sanskrit reconstruction or translation.
- Temple-specific movement, timing and pronunciation remain under the officiant's guidance. The new “what to attend to” passages are study guidance, not invented ritual instructions.
